Title: Learning to Recommend in Unknown Games

Abstract:
We study preference learning through recommendations in multi-agent game settings, where a moderator repeatedly interacts with agents whose utility functions are unknown. In each round, the moderator commits to a recommendation mechanism, privately suggests actions, and observes whether agents follow or deviate. We consider two canonical behavioral feedback models — best response and quantal response — and characterize what each reveals about the underlying game.

We establish a learnability separation: under quantal-response feedback, the game utilities are identifiable up to a positive affine equivalence class with logarithmic sample complexity, whereas best-response feedback admits a strictly larger indistinguishability set, which we fully characterize via the normal fan structure of utility polytopes. We introduce a regret notion based on agents' incentives to deviate from recommendations and construct an online cutting-plane algorithm achieving O(nM log T) cumulative regret under both feedback models, where n is the number of agents and M the size of the joint action space. We further show that under a mild rationality threshold, compliant agents achieve O(nM log T) swap regret — in contrast to the Ω(√T) lower bound for decentralized no-regret learners — establishing a formal incentive for agents to follow the moderator.

We validate the theoretical predictions with computational experiments on games with up to 12 agents, demonstrating convergence to correlated equilibria and the gap between centralized and decentralized learning.
